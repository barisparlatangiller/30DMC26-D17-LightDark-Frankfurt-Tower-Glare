Towers cast shade. Glass towers also throw the sun back.

  1. Dark is the shadow of every building of 30 m and more, on the ground and on the real roofs around it (as in Day 5).
  2. Light is sunlight bounced off glass façades. A glass wall works like a mirror: the sun leaves it at the same angle it arrived and lands on streets and roofs, often on the side of the tower that is not in the sun.
  3. Blue towers have glass façades, from A1 (full mirror-like glass) to C (mostly stone). Every tower of 58 m and more was checked façade by façade.

Brightest mirrors · 21 Jun

    Method, data & limits

    How the reflections are made

    A glass wall is a mirror. Sunlight that falls on a vertical façade at a height of α degrees leaves it at the same α, going down, mirrored across the wall. So each façade throws a "reverse shadow": a patch of light on the side the sun comes from, on the ground and on the roofs below.

    Blocked both ways. A façade point only reflects when it is itself in the sun, and its light only lands where nothing is in the way. Both tests run over the same 2 m surface of LoD2 roofs and ground as the shadows, so towers, low buildings and the terrain all block.

    Façade classes. Every tower of 58 m and more (Wikipedia's list, 81 towers) was checked one by one in its article text and lead photo, and classed: A1 full mirror-like glass (weight 1.0), A2 full lighter glass or double skin (0.75), B mixed: window bands, metal or stone frames (0.45), C mostly stone, brick or matt metal (0.15), U not verified (drawn apart). The weights are relative assumptions, not measured reflectance.

    Matching. Inventory towers are joined to LoD2 buildings by their OpenStreetMap name, or else by coordinate and height. Every outer wall of a matched tower is sampled every 1.5 m.

    Sun. pvlib (NREL SPA), the 21st of each month 2026, every 30 minutes while the sun is at least 5° high, as Day 5.

    How the shadows are made (Day 5)

    Every LoD2 building part of 30 m and more casts a shadow onto the real surfaces around it: every roof at its own LoD2 height and the ground between them, following the LoD2 roof planes. Low buildings receive shadow but cast none. 2 m grid in a frame turned towards the sun.

    Data quality & limits

    Façades are treated as flat vertical mirrors with one weight per tower; real glass differs by coating, angle (more reflective at grazing angles), frames and open windows. Curved towers are drawn from their LoD2 outline, so their light fans out. Light that hits a wall is drawn where it meets the roof edge. Clear sky is assumed. The map shows where reflected sunlight can land, not measured glare, brightness or temperature.

    New towers that are not yet in the LoD2 model (Four, ONE, The Spin, Eden, FAZ-Tower and others) cast no shadow and reflect no light here.
